# Battle Replay

## Purpose and mode

Operate. Help the account owner choose one saved Battle Log and follow its recorded actions on the approved visual board. Battle Replay (Beta) is a derived read view, not a saved record, editor, simulator, or public share. Product meaning lives in `PRODUCT.md` and `CONTEXT.md`; visual authority remains in `docs/design-system.md`. Preserve the reviewed prototype composition and assets rather than opening a new identity exercise.

## Built direction

The `/replays` picker sits inside the authenticated app shell with the shared collection header and flat whole-row links. Each row identifies the matchup and actual saved date, result, format, and turns. Latest games and Older games retain the existing bounded owner-log window. Keep loading and empty states in that hierarchy, with a direct path to Battle Logs.

The `/replays/[id]` viewer uses the wide shared page container. Its page header provides View Battle Log and Choose another game. Replay metadata leads into the approved timeline/board composition. The opponent is above the player; Prize cards sit left, Active Pokémon centrally, Bench beneath Active, and Deck/Discard piles right. Preserve hands, player-owned stadium placement, transaction overlays, evolution stacks, condition markers, attached Energy, damage, coin tosses, and KO motion. Keep the existing neutral app surfaces and typography around this functional board; replay-specific card treatment does not become a system for unrelated pages.

The board mirrors the two fields with the same Active/Bench gap: the opponent’s Bench is above its Active, and the player’s Bench is below. Prizes use one card back with the remaining count beneath it; Prize, Deck and Discard cards share their responsive size and label treatment. Hand labels sit above the opponent’s cards and below the player’s. Opening hands use separated cards and appear once. The last setup position finishes placement, reveals opening Pokémon and holds the board before the first turn draw, without adding a source action or changing deep-link positions.

Action, Play/Pause and Turn controls sit in the opponent’s upper-right header. Replay position stays below the board, followed by Partial replay diagnostics. Recorded source remains accessible through View Battle Log. Ordinary placements, promotions, retreats and switches move recorded physical cards on the board; routine single-card turn draws move Deck to Hand, proven manual Energy attachments move Hand to the Pokémon, and simple Stadium placements move Hand to the owner’s Stadium slot. A recorded replacement may discard the previous Stadium within that routine placement. Their wording sits centrally. Stadium activations, additional effects, and unresolved movements retain their transaction context. Search/effect interactions such as Poffin and Crispin retain transaction context above the board from the first animation frame; transformed or elevated board cards stay inside the board’s stacking context. Seeking, resizing and reduced motion settle transient movement without changing the compiled state.

Attack names appear once in the centre announcement, while the attacking Pokémon retains its pulse and recorded damage markers. Stadium artwork and names fit a reserved lane immediately to the left of the owning Active through desktop and tablet widths. Only replay containers of 28rem or less share the left lane with Prizes while Active remains in the central field. Stadium never occupies a row below Active or the gap between both Actives. Neither Stadium nor its label may overlap the Active card or its attachments.

Card previews show artwork and HP. Attached Energy icons stay readable, and representative Energy cards match their Pokémon’s size and top/bottom alignment, exposing their right edge behind it. When a complete source-compatible allocation survives bounded reconstruction, damage, positions and attachments may use one consistent best-guess history. Show its numeric values plainly, qualify the card's accessible label, explain the choice in its preview, and provide one expandable board summary. The fact frames and proof plans stay separate. Otherwise, unassigned attachments remain in the existing evidence pools. The current presentation policy is in `docs/agents/battle-replay-presentation.md`.

Card artwork fits in full without cropping or stretching. Preserve the source image’s original borders and corners rather than applying rounded masks to board, Hand, Energy, effect, discard or preview artwork.

Desktop Bench slots have wider horizontal gutters and reserve space after the last card for attached Energy. Bench Energy is tucked closer than Active Energy while leaving its colour visible; expanded six-to-eight-slot Benches use the full play-area width to keep cards readable. Narrow layouts retain their existing compact spacing.

The sidebar entry sits beside Battle Logs. Beta remains visible in expanded/mobile navigation and uses the active navigation foreground when selected. The explicit accessible link name and icon-rail tooltip are Battle Replay (Beta). Preserve the existing Friends pending-request dot and its separate count description.

## Responsive bounds

Use the named `replay` inline-size container so layout responds to the actual width left by the sidebar. Above 68rem, timeline and board are side by side. At 68rem or less, the board and controls lead and the timeline follows with a bounded scrolling list. At 44rem or less, matchup metadata reflows. At 40rem or less, preserve the approved mobile Active-card size, prizes/field/piles layout, full-width Bench, and enlarged known hand cards. The pile-width custom property remains local and responsive.

Hand rows scroll within their available width and reserve room for the reviewed hover/focus lift. Energy sizing, health spacing, and all later mobile overrides remain part of the approved baseline. The discard sheet is portaled outside the replay container and therefore retains its separate 44rem viewport breakpoint. Card preview remains bounded by viewport width and dynamic height.

## Source and privacy contract

Both routes require the current user; selected logs are read through the existing account-scoped `getLogById` path. The picker uses the current owner's log window. Replay compilation receives the saved source sections and produces a serializable program, one event and resulting frame per recorded action plus an initial frame. Events retain `sourceSectionIndex` so both players' same-numbered turns remain distinct. The client receives frames and events without executable mutation functions. No personal pilot fixtures, sample-player selectors, external iframe, new database table, or schema migration belongs to this integration.

Replaying never changes or resaves the Battle Log. IDs and position query values are bounded before use; unavailable/non-owner records remain unavailable. Replay is owner-only and does not expand shared-log, Friendship, or Player Profile access.

## Uncertainty and artwork

Keep recorded actions distinct and traceable. Unknown HP and hand, deck, or Prize counts remain labelled; hidden cards stay hidden. In the enlarged preview, HP unknown sits on an opaque `--card` surface with `--card-foreground` text, a `--border` stroke, and `--dialog-radius-compact`; its legibility must not depend on the artwork or overlay beneath it. Partial reconstruction shows its diagnostic explanation, affected incomplete zones, unknown accumulated damage, and unresolved action notes alongside the recorded source. Each source line is applied transactionally: an unresolved line invalidates the affected knowledge while independently established transitions in the same action remain visible. Contradictory counts become unknown rather than preventing a later explicit named movement. Later frames retain uncertainty until the source re-establishes the affected fact. An unsupported log offers its original written Battle Log instead of presenting an invented board.

Bounded reconciliation may carry proven later source evidence back through the same physical card’s earlier valid history, including evolution layers, recovery, Hand, board, discard and transaction previews. A physical card’s identity is separate from its stack or projected board position; an evolved top and the card beneath it have distinct identities. Tool attachments, damage and positional uncertainty do not erase independently proven printing facts. Actual identity uncertainty and contradictions stop propagation. Unknown copy assignments preserve proven facts as an unordered multiset rather than sharing them across namesakes. Evidence from an evolved top never establishes the earlier Pokémon’s printing. Native effects may prove a compatible printing family without proving its exact illustration. If the original copy is unidentified but its Active position remains uninterrupted, native proof may narrow that Active episode alone; any switch, promotion, top change or identity-changing source gap ends the interval. This position proof never identifies an original pooled copy or spreads to its namesakes. When a copy lacks its own printing evidence, its probable HP and artwork may prefer a compatible printing family already witnessed for the same player in this game, after linked-list and native evidence. Conflicting witnessed families leave the generic fallback in place; this preference never changes semantic card rules or physical-copy proof. Probable display choices remain separate from proof and cannot prune physical alternatives or establish an exact played printing. Unresolved alternatives retain their uncertainty.

Unambiguous existing public catalog rows may classify a recorded Ability, attack or Trainer type and establish an explicitly stated single movement origin. Missing, conflicting or unsupported effect text leaves that origin unknown. This does not execute card text or infer an unrecorded effect. Catalog lookup is bounded and failure leaves the source available with unresolved changes.

Display the actual saved date and result. Existing card artwork may be a representative printing, with a visible notice and no legality assertion. Missing mappings may use a bounded public printing lookup for already visible names, requiring the complete canonical card name to match. Lookup and image failures show the card name and Artwork unavailable on the board, hand, transaction, discard, and enlarged preview surfaces. Attached Energy retains each recorded type and count when grouped; Special Energy is never assigned a guessed basic type. A known attached Tool is shown as a full card behind its Pokémon, aligned at the top and bottom and exposed on the left while Energy stays on the right. Both attachment previews are labelled and clickable. A Tool whose current position is unresolved may be displayed behind a plausible holder only within the selected complete surviving history, with an accessible qualification and preview explanation. Its pooled evidence is retained separately from this display allocation; when no complete history can be projected safely, the assignment evidence panel remains visible. Official rulebook Energy symbols and Burn/Poison markers retain their provenance; the custom Sleep Zzz and existing prototype coin artwork retain their documented origins. Paralyzed and Confused use distinct labelled icon markers.

## Interaction and states

Preserve action and turn stepping, Play/Pause, Restart, the range scrubber, and bounded position deep links. Turn controls stop at each source section's first recorded action rather than combining the players' same-numbered turns. Seeking pauses playback. Opening a preview or discard inspection also pauses playback. Dismissing either returns focus to its originating control while that control remains present. A preview opened inside the discard gallery returns focus to that gallery card; dismissing the gallery then returns focus to the board's discard control. Changing the selected saved log remounts the keyed renderer and resets playback, dialogs, and position. A selected timeline action can bring the board into view in the stacked layout.

Use shared Buttons, typography, and `AppDialogContent`, with visible focus and accessible labels. Current-action updates use a polite live region. Reduced motion keeps state transitions understandable without the full animation. Error recovery uses the installed Next error boundary API and retains links to the picker or original log. Loading, empty, partial, unsupported, and unavailable states are distinct.
