## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The product content is unmistakably Poke Notes: matchup sprites, turn order, placements, deck records, mulligans, prizing, and the 60-card calculator all speak directly to competitive players. Underneath, the visual grammar remains a fairly interchangeable dark analytics dashboard. Battle Logs feels most authored; Stats and Tournaments could be reskinned for another sports product. The missed opportunity is interaction specificity: the app records evidence convincingly but rarely turns it into a comparison, prompt, or next decision.

**Deterministic scan:** The source-target CLI scan returned zero findings. The live detector reported 28 route-level signals across five rendered pages: Logs 4, Decks 6, Tournaments 9, Stats 6, Calculator 3. Actionable signals were a skipped heading level in `LogsManager`, low-contrast “2nd” text in `LogRow`, and broad layout-property transitions in the shared sidebar. Most nested-card warnings on ledger rows and segmented controls, plus Calculator’s flat-type-hierarchy warning, were false positives because visual grouping and title dominance remain clear.

**Visual overlays:** Injection succeeded in a hidden fresh in-app-browser tab on all five routes, but the browser cannot expose sub-agent tabs to the user. No reliable user-visible overlay remains; screenshots, DOM snapshots, computed styles, overlay text, and console logs were used as fallback evidence.

## Overall Impression

This is a credible, serious competitive tool with a coherent shell and unusually good domain language. Its biggest opportunity is to graduate from a clean record system into a decision-support workspace: retain the calm visual system, but make mobile comparisons, archive retrieval, analytical scope, and “what this means” substantially stronger.

## What's Working

- Battle Logs is the strongest product-authored surface. Date chunking, matchup sprites, result labels, turn order, and opponent framing create a fast competitive scan.
- The shared shell is disciplined: visible navigation labels, semantic headings, active states, a skip link, chart alternative text, and text alongside outcome colors.
- Desktop Stats has excellent immediate hierarchy. Results and Damage ledgers make headline numbers effortless to parse.

## Cognitive Load

Five of eight checks fail, concentrated in Deck Strategy and Calculator: chunking, one-thing-at-a-time, minimal choices, working-memory support, and progressive disclosure. The largest decision sets are the five global routes plus import, five deck sort controls plus 17 deck destinations, the date-group archive, and roughly 28 calculator card tiles followed by long tables. Grouping and primary hierarchy remain strong on Logs, Tournaments, and desktop Stats.

## Emotional Journey

The first impression is competence: sober dark surfaces, direct numbers, and domain imagery. The peaks are recognizable matchups and clear records. The valleys arrive when a 559-log archive has no retrieval layer, mobile Deck Strategy loses its comparison evidence, Stats shows totals without interpretation, and Calculator puts many screens of input proof before the odds a player came to understand. Most route endings display data but do not close with a useful next move.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Mobile Deck Strategy removes the reason the page exists**
   - **Why it matters:** Only sprites and names remain; users must open and remember multiple decks to compare performance.
   - **Fix:** Keep record/win rate and recency in a compact two-line row; move only secondary fields behind expansion.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

2. **[P1] Calculator prioritizes input proof over decision value**
   - **Why it matters:** On mobile, the 60-card graphic occupies many screens before streak and per-card probabilities.
   - **Fix:** Lead with probability summary and Setup/Prizing analysis; collapse or tab the visual deck audit; add results jump navigation.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

3. **[P1] Stats reports totals without supporting strategic decisions**
   - **Why it matters:** “All time” is the only visible scope, and cumulative damage mainly confirms that more games create more damage.
   - **Fix:** Add deck, format, opponent/archetype, and date filters; emphasize rates, deltas, and notable matchup signals.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

4. **[P2] The Battle Log archive lacks retrieval tools**
   - **Why it matters:** A 559-log collection depends on collapsed dates and Load More, with no visible search or filters.
   - **Fix:** Add compact search/filter controls and state the currently loaded date window.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

5. **[P2] Contextual interpretation and small accessibility details lag the visual system**
   - **Why it matters:** Metrics lack definitions or next-step guidance; the live detector also found low-contrast “2nd” text and an h1-to-h3 heading jump.
   - **Fix:** Add concise metric/methodology help, raise the muted placement contrast, correct date-group heading semantics, and narrow sidebar transitions to the properties that actually animate.
   - **Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex — power user:** No visible keyboard accelerators; 559 logs lack fast retrieval; Deck Strategy has no saved view or quick comparison; Calculator requires long scrolling instead of direct jumps to Setup or Prizing.

**Sam — keyboard/screen-reader/low-vision:** The skip link, labels, headings, tablists, chart alternative text, and non-color outcome labels are strong. Risks remain in the skipped Logs heading level, low-contrast placement text, missing mobile deck metrics, and repeated “1 copies” accessible wording. Full keyboard traversal, live-region behavior, 200% zoom, and light-theme contrast were not exhaustively tested.

**Casey — distracted mobile user:** Mobile Logs is well chunked, but Deck Strategy requires repeated open/back comparison, Stats spends too much vertical space per metric, and Calculator has no progress marker or jump navigation through its oversized result.

## Minor Observations

- “All time” looks like a filter but exposes no alternatives.
- “Clear Deck” has equal red prominence to the primary Import action.
- Mobile Logs hides opponent handle and turn order, reducing retrieval cues.
- Tournament History leaves a large inert page tail with only four events.
- Several Calculator card artworks were still blank after a short wait; treat this as a latency lead, not a confirmed defect.
- Development toolbar UI was excluded from the critique.

- Which three deck facts must never disappear on mobile?
- Should the 60-card graphic be the calculator centerpiece, or collapsible proof that parsing succeeded?
- Should Battle Logs evolve from a chronological archive into a retrieval and review workspace?
